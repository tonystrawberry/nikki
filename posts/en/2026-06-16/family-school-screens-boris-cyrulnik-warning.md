Interview with psychiatrist and neuropsychiatrist **Boris Cyrulnik** on a form of **decivilization** he sees in Western society — centered on family, school, screens, and the erosion of empathy.

## Culture vs civilization

- **Culture** — the stable layer: laws, social organization, institutions (school, marriage, architecture)
- **Civilization** — conventions that evolve: language, politeness rituals, turn-taking, what counts as "civilized" behavior

Civilization is arbitrary and changes under ecological, social, and verbal pressure — like language itself. Montaigne's example: French Christians were horrified by cannibal Indians; the Indians found the French barbaric for leaving the poor to die in the streets. Each group had rituals that let them live together morally — but different rituals.

**Religion** is a major shaper of civilization: it gives codes for how men and women should behave, often hierarchical (dominion over animals, women, children, the weak).

## Signs of a civilization "taking on water"

Civilization is **education and language that teach mastery of impulse**.

Neuroimaging shows why early deprivation matters:

- Lack of affective and sensory stimulation in early childhood causes **brain dysfunction**
- In boys especially, insufficient speech around the baby can lead to **atrophy of the prefrontal lobes**
- The prefrontal cortex inhibits the **amygdala** — the neurological base of impulse, hatred, anger
- Without that inhibition, boys are more driven by impulse: sexual desire or anger moves straight to action

Girls tend to speak earlier and often learn impulse control more easily.

**Romanian orphanages** (200,000 children deprived of family): frightening neurological atrophy. **The home sculpts the brain.**

## The first 1000 days

The critical window runs from **pregnancy through the emergence of speech** (~3 years).

Old medical/psychology teaching wrongly assumed children understand nothing before they speak — now known to be false. These preverbal years form what could be called the child's **temperament**:

- Do I take pleasure in approaching others?
- Am I afraid of others?

Family and society organize these 1000 days. When children are isolated:

- **Boys** tend toward violence
- **Girls** tend toward self-aggression (Cyrulnik saw 2–3 scarification cases in 40 years of practice; young psychiatrists today report staggering rates among women)

## Family ruptures and the "sprint civilization"

Our natural tranquilizer is **familiarity**. Constant ruptures anxiety children who never have time to internalize a secure sense of home.

A child's day today is **endless context-switching** — changing environments constantly. A young person may have **4–5 partners and 4–5 careers** in a lifetime → repeated tears and ruptures. Cyrulnik has worked with children who had **five stepfathers** — where is the family structure?

More women want a baby for **individual fulfillment**, not to build a family or raise "soldiers" (a model still strong in some Middle Eastern cultures he works in).

**School** gave women power to become persons rather than being sacrificed to husband and children — individual progress, but populationally:

- More divorce and separation
- Family bonds less securing; parents may have one child in the US, another in Japan — links diluted
- Before: family could be stifling; now: **too diluted** — freed, we are increasingly alone

Every civilizational progress has a price.

## Screens: magical machines with a cost

Screens are miraculous — a Zoom call puts 400 people in a 10 m² office. But above **3–4 hours of screen time per day**, **depression risk is multiplied by 4**.

## Progress always has side effects

Examples Cyrulnik uses:

| Progress | Side effect |
|----------|-------------|
| **Cesarean section** | Saved countless mothers; now some schedule it for vacation — surgical intervention, less natural passage for the baby; "little Caesars" may have more infections (beneficial microbes from the birth canal) |
| **Childbirth hygiene (Semmelweis)** | Women's life expectancy soared (was ~36 years after ~13 pregnancies in the 19th century) |
| **Girls in school** | Independence, less need for marriage as survival; women choose partners; family bonds less constraining — also aging population, fewer children |
| **Post-war optimism** | Naïve belief that medicine, psychoanalysis, and social progress would fix everything — every advance has secondary effects |

## Self-development culture vs empathy

Cyrulnik avoids "egocentrism" (which still implies knowing what others want). He speaks of a **self-development civilization**:

- My fulfillment, my sport, my grades, my social performance
- In a **sprint civilization**, we stop noticing others' inner worlds → **empathy shuts down**

Same mechanism as in war or totalitarian regimes: stop empathizing to act. In peacetime you cannot kill the other; in war, killing becomes celebration. He hears today phrases he heard in the late 1930s and during WWII.

Self-development culture has a similar effect: I have things to do, I flourish, I take care of myself — **whatever price others pay**. Society holds together, but it is an **anxious society**.

## Making civilization more livable

Possible levers:

- **Protect the first 1000 days** — prevent early brain dysfunction (easy to say, often not done)
- **Change school rhythms** — Japan had unbearable child suicide rates (partly blamed on "second school" cram culture); now banning second school, slowing pace and grading. Finland: **first grades at 11** — "you didn't get it yet, you'll get it tomorrow." Children don't learn linearly but in **steps** (don't understand → don't understand → click). Slowing pace **improves** results; what drives good learning is **pleasure in playing at learning**. Constraint teaches poorly. Children suffer more at school today despite less physical punishment — because they are **over-invested** (3% went to lycée in his generation; ~95% now pursue the bac)
- **Reinvent small civilizational rituals** — theatre troupes, walking clubs, lifelong learning (80-year-olds at university)
- **Develop empathy through art** — comment on literature, films, theatre as ancient Greece did. When empathy grows, **difference interests me**; in totalitarian culture, difference is contemptible

## Key Takeaways

- **Culture** (institutions) and **civilization** (conventions) both shape how we live; civilization evolves faster and can erode empathy
- The **first 1000 days** (pregnancy to speech) sculpt temperament and brain structure — especially critical for boys' impulse control
- **Constant family and environmental ruptures** anxiety children; diluted family bonds leave people alone despite "freedom"
- **Screen time > 3–4 h/day** quadruples depression risk — progress always has a bill
- **School acceleration** and over-investment hurt children even without corporal punishment
- Empathy can be rebuilt through **ritual, art, and slower rhythms** — difference as curiosity, not contempt
