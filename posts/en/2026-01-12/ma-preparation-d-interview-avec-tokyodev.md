# Why did I come to Japan?

- I was looking for a job in Montreal but I decided to take a chance and come here!
- I was always a fan of Japan (anime, manga, culture, food, etc.) but I had no specific plans to live here
- It was God's plan. Found the job offer on Facebook by chance.
- I've been here ever since, found my life partner and I really enjoy it here!

# Why did you decide to join Spacely?

- I wanted to go back to in-house development so I could focus on building a product with more depth and ownership.
- As a tech lead in my previous job, my role was to focus on leading the projects at a higher level which means lots of meeting, lots of code reviews and mentoring.
- But I wanted to get closer to coding and building the product and get my hands dirty more.
- The product looked very interesting and unique (usage of VR for real estate, virtual home staging, etc.)
- The balance here is perfect for me between doing feature tasks and health tasks.
- Remote work. I feel way more productive and focused at home.
- The work-life balance is very good here. I like my work but I also enjoy my time outside of work. I really value this balance.
- I feel Spacely is a very fun environment to work in. I like the fact that you can do anything (as long as it is benefiting the company). You have very little constraints and it's a very open environment where you can propose new ideas and projects.
- But this is all thanks to the people working here. Since I joined here, everyone is so friendly and supportive. Even though we all work remotely, I feel the members are very close and we all get along well.

# Can you tell me more about your role? I.e. job title, what you work on and how your team works?

- I am a Team Lead Backend Engineer. Moved from being a member to a leader.
- Focus on the Ruby on Rails backend of our web application (developing new features, maintaining the codebase, performance monitoring, bug fixes). Also do some frontend and infrastructure tasks (AWS) when needed.
- Develop new features in close collaboration with PMs and other teams (Frontend, Mobile, R&D, Infra)
- Since becoming a team lead, I also handle managerial tasks like leading team meetings (daily standups, sprint planning, sprint reviews, etc.), reporting to project managers, and handling recruitment (interviews and coding assessment reviews) and onboarding new members.
- We try to have less meetings as possible and focus on delivering value to the business.
- The originality of the VR product made me come here but I actually don't work on the VR part at all.
- But Spacely is a very flexible company and you can ask to be assigned to other teams if you really want to.
- Recently, an iOS engineer asked to switch to the backend team and we were able to welcome him to the team. We were at a phase where we were short on backend engineers so it was a win-win situation.
- All engineers (BE, FE, Mobile, R&D) are assigned to "projects". We have for example the "VR player project", the "mobile app feature project", the "data analytics project" etc. Each project has a PM and a team lead as well as members.
- Engineers are quite autonomous at Spacely. No one is going to give you instructions on what/how to do.

# Talk me through the development process at Spacely. I.e. how do you decide what to work on, what's the process to shipping it?

- PMs decide what features we need to work on next. We have a roadmap and we already know what features we are going to work on for the next 6 months
- We have a meeting per project every two weeks to discuss the ideas, the progress, the blockers, the next steps...
- Engineers are responsible for breaking down the tasks, estimating them, evaluating the complexity, etc.
- We use Notion to write the specifications before starting to work on a feature. It helps us to have a clear understanding of the feature and share it with the team, as well as using it as input for the AI coding agent (we are trying to use Notion MCP server to accelerate the development process)
- There are releases every day. We are using the classic dev/staging/production environments.

# What is the most fun or creative part of your job?

- The good thing is that there is not only ONE thing that I find it fun here. For me, developing itself and imagining how to build a new feature is fun. But also refactoring code and making it cleaner and more maintainable is also very fun. I feel very good after doing it.
- Spacely has a rule where we dedicate 30% of our time to "health tasks" (refactoring and technical debt). This is a very good thing because it allows us to keep the codebase clean and maintainable.
- But since I took over some of the managerial tasks, even though I thought I would not like it at first, I actually enjoy it. I feel it's a good skill to have, to manage people, to be good at communication and leading meetings and be able to know what/who to delegate tasks to. I am mentoring some new joiners recently and I like it.
- At Spacely, you can just focus on your tasks at hand but you can also go a bit out of the way and propose new ideas (improving the knowledge base, adding some coding guidelines, automating some tasks, etc.). You can ask for challenges.

# What has been the most challenging thing?

- Catching up with the codebase as a new joiner. The amount of code is quite big and for a startup (of now 8 years) with few members, it is quite necessary to have a bird eye view and a global understanding of how it works.

# What do you feel Spacely does that sets them apart from other companies?

- A very open and friendly culture. The people.
- No blame culture. We support each other, even in cases of failure.
- Even though it's a Japanese company, the engineering team is very international. We have members from South Korea, China, Switzerland, Sweden, United States, France, Philippines and United Kingdom. We do kanji quizzes daily on slack where we share some difficult kanjis with each other.
- A very flexible work environment. We are not tied to a fixed schedule. You can work from home or the office (in Shibuya) and you can start early and finish early (or the opposite).
- A very good work-life balance. Very few overtime work as far as I am concerned. The important thing is to get the job done.
- Some people have part-time jobs (副業) with other companies. It's allowed here.
- Personally, I spend a lot of my time working on a Japanese learning app (shirimono.fun) after work or on weekends. That is to say I am not feeling burnt out at all.
- Also, some people in the company took their paternity leave for around 6 months and we were able to cover their tasks without any problem. So yeah, if you have a baby here, don't worry, we will take care of you.
- Using AI a lot in the product features and in the development process and a lot of financial support (for remote work equipment, Japanese lessons/books, certification fees, tech events, etc.).
- I can say Spacely is able to offer you challenges and opportunities to grow if you are willing to take them.
- My CTO John said "be bold and take risks. Better try to do something and fail than to not try at all".
