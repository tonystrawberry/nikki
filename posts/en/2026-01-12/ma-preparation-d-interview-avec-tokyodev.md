Tomorrow, I will be interviewed by TokyoDev to introduce myself and my work at Spacely.
I am not so good at interviews so I need to prepare some questions beforehand lol

# Why did I come to Japan?

My arrival in Japan was actually completely unplanned. Back in 2018, I was about to graduate from university in Canada and found a job offer for a French company based in Tokyo on Facebook by chance.

I was originally looking for a job in Montreal, but I decided to take a chance and come here! I was always a fan of Japan, but I had no specific plans to live here. I guess it was fate (or God's plan). I’ve been here ever since and I really enjoy it!

# Why did you decide to join Spacely?

Before Spacely, I was working as a Backend Tech Lead at a big consulting firm. While I learned a lot there, client work often meant tight deadlines where we had to prioritize delivery speed over code quality.

I eventually reached a point where I wanted to go back to in-house development so I could focus on building a product with more depth and ownership. Also, as a tech lead, my role was to focus on leading the projects at a higher level which means lots of meeting, lots of code reviews and mentoring, even though I volunteered to also partipate in the development. But I wanted to get my hands dirty more. I like coding. That’s why I joined Spacely. The product fascinated me—especially the VR player and virtual home staging.

Spacely main product is a platform for real estate agents to manage their properties and their clients. Properties can be viewable in a 3D viewer on the website or using VR headsets. One use case for example is to allow potential home buyers/renters to view a property in a 3D viewer before deciding to visit it. It's also possible to view non-yet built properties (3D models of the property), enhance with furnitures, manage clients with a CRM etc.

I also really appreciate the balance here. We move fast, but we also have that 30% rule where we dedicate time in every sprint to refactoring and bug fixing. That commitment to quality, combined with the remote work culture and small team size, made it the perfect fit for me.

# Can you tell me more about your role? I.e. job title, what you work on and how your team works?

I’m the Team Lead Backend Engineer at Spacely. As the title suggests, I focus on the backend of our web application. This involves developing new features in close collaboration with PMs and other teams (Frontend, Mobile, R&D), as well as maintaining the codebase through performance monitoring and bug fixes.

Beyond the code, I handle managerial tasks like leading team meetings, reporting to project managers, and handling hiring and onboarding. Working at a startup requires versatility—you have to be present on all fronts. I’ve felt trusted by my team since day one, which has been a huge motivation for me to grow and do good work.

As for our process, we follow the Scrum framework with two-week sprints. We strictly allocate 70% of our time to feature development and 30% to 'health tasks' (refactoring and technical debt). We have short daily standups to align on progress, and while individual members own specific projects, we emphasize cross-reviewing code. This ensures knowledge is shared across the team rather than being siloed with one person.

# Talk me through the development process at Spacely. I.e. how do you decide what to work on, what's the process to shipping it?

At Spacely, PMs are the ones who decide what to work on next. They come up with the ideas and we have a meeting every two weeks to discuss them (feasibility, priority, schedule, etc.). We have a roadmap and we already know what features we are going to work on for the next 6 months.

Then, when we actually start working on a feature, we break down the tasks and define them in Jira, our project management tool. So we create the EPIC task and them have sub-tasks assigned to the team members (FE, BE, Mobile, R&D).

We also use Notion to write the specifications before starting to work on a feature. It helps us to have a clear understanding of the feature and share it with the team, so that everyone is on the same page and asks for adjustments if needed. We are planning to make use of MCP server coupled with coding agent for accelerating the development process.

After that, we get our hands dirty and start coding. We use Slack a lot to communicate with the team, share our progress, ask for help or clarifications. Everyone is very reactive at Spacely so when you have a question, you can ask it directly and you will get an answer quickly.

So when we want to ship a feature, we need to get it tested by the Customer Success and the Product Manager on the staging environment. They are the ones who are responsible for the customer satisfaction and they are the ones who will test the feature and give us the feedback.

Once the feature is tested and approved, we can ship it to the production environment. We use GitHub Actions to deploy the code to the production environment which is hosted on AWS.

# What is the most fun or creative part of your job?

Well, the thing that I like here is that you can do anything (as long as it is benefiting the company). You have very little constraints and it's a very open environment where you can propose new ideas and projects.

If you want to work on MCP server, you can do it. You want to improve a certain parts of the application not related to the features you are assigned to? It's fine! You want to propose a new way of writing a knowledge base? Just ask and you will get the support you need. Actually, in the past year since my arrival, I tried to propose some improvements (at the technical but also the organizational level) and most of them have been adopted. Everyone has been so supportive and very flexible. Want to work on the infra side as well? Consult the infra team and they will help you to get started.

I like that I can get my hands dirty here and work closely to the code, developing new features, imagining how to build a new product. I also love refactoring code and making it more maintainable and readable by all (especially for new joiners). Spacely is now 8 year old and as every code base, it has some flaws so I feel very motivated to improve it and make it better for the future.

# What has been the most challenging thing?

Catching up with the codebase and understanding the business logic. As I said, Spacely is now 8 year old and it's not so easy to understand the codebase when you are new, right? Some parts were not so well documented, so you are not very sure the real intention behind the code sometimes BUT this is improving at a good pace. We are adopting continuous improvement so we try to leave the code in a better state than we found it.

And balancing time between feature tasks and health tasks. We have a 70/30 rule where we dedicate 70% of our time to feature tasks and 30% to health tasks. But it's never exactly 70/30. We tend to have more feature tasks of course because this is what the business needs but we end up delaying the health tasks when we are short on time.

# What do you feel Spacely does that sets them apart from other companies?

A very open culture. Everyone is very friendly and supportive, for real. I speak only for myself, but I feel we all get along quite well.
A very flexible work environment. We are not tied to a fixed schedule. You can work from home, you can work from the office, you can work from anywhere you want.
A very good work-life balance. We are not working 12 hours a day, most of the time we are not working overtime. It is even possible to work part-time (副業) if you want to for another company.

And we are trying to use AI a lot in the product features (some of our recent features involves using AI for generating furnitures for real estate images for example, or for generating captions for images based on the real estate information in the database) and in the development process.

# Why would you recommend another developer join Spacely?

Well, I described a lot of the good things about Spacely in the previous questions. I would say that the main reason to join Spacely is the open culture and the flexibility of the work environment.
